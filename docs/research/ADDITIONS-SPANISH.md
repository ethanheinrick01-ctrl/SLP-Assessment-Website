## Numerical evidence and limits

PLS normative n1,150 is directly publisher-supported; monolingual/bilingual and U.S./Puerto Rico composition is retained. Dialect, exposure, and monolingual/bilingual subgroup counts remain unverified. [De Anda et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9132146/), Measures/PLS-5 Spanish, gives manual reliability summaries without complete score/age/method/sample mappings. These were not converted into verified coefficients.

PLS accuracy summaries in [Peredo et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8765734/) (Screening Measures/PLS-5 Spanish) and [Hernández et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11021047/) (Introduction/total-language assessment) differ and lack the matched source-table context needed here. No PLS accuracy pair is displayed.

[Barragan et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5963037/), Method/Determination of Language Ability and Results/Question 2, supplies original CELF-4 Spanish cohort accuracy. The retained pair is 93%/65% at Core SS85; the Abstract says 94%, so this conflict is visible. The same cohort's exploratory SS78 pair is 86%/80%, not a universal clinical rule. Classification n293 comprises 28 impaired/265 typical after six exclusions. Converging reference measures used Spanish SSLIC/narrative and English SPELT-3. Low-income Spanish-dominant ages 5–7 in English-only Arizona schools defines applicability. The paper reports the manual normative n1,019; direct manual inspection remains outstanding.

Spanish internal, retest, scorer, and total-score tables remain unverified. CELF administration time also remains unverified in the checked current primary publisher sources. Fields preserve null methodological/sample data and explicit not-reported status.
