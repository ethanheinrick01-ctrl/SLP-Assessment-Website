## Interpretation and remaining gaps

**PPVT-5.** The publisher's .97 summary and the review's mean split-half .97 are separate summaries. The review reports .94–.98 split-half, mean retest .84 (n213), and mean alternate-form .86 (n273); publisher summaries give retest .88 and alternate-form .84. The infographic's .98/.98/.95 values are negative predictive power at 10% prevalence, not specificity. A matched sensitivity/specificity pair remains unavailable from those sources. Root also rechecked its three-correct basal, six-incorrect ceiling, and one permitted repetition against publisher materials.

**CELF-5.** Publisher norm total is 2,380. The graphic supplies scorer estimates .99 Word Structure, .95 Formulated Sentences, .91 Word Definitions, and .96 Structured Writing. Accuracy varies with cutoff: .97/.97 at SS80, 1.00/.91 at SS85. It is not one universal accuracy rate. Recalling Sentences reversal, four-zero stopping rule, and no-repetition instruction were rechecked in the existing local manual text, printed pp. 26 and 74; no manual or test items are redistributed.

**PLS-5 English.** Current publisher time is 45–60 minutes; the older brochure says 30–60. The review's normative split-half values are AC .91, EC .93, Total Language .95; these differ in aggregation from an age-specific .80–.97 range. Retest .86–.95 uses n195 and 3–28 days. Examiner agreement .96–.99 uses n54; 91.9–100% scoring agreement concerns 200 protocols. Accuracy .83/.80 at SS85 retains the publisher/review age-label disagreement. The basal/ceiling also agrees with [Hsiao et al.'s original study procedures](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9132022/); Pearson's FAQ confirms use of the lowest basal and ceiling. The repetition exceptions reported in Sami's study remain study procedures rather than an assertion of a fully checked U.S. manual rule.

**GFTA-3.** Technical graphic alpha ranges .94–.97 across sex/task groups; the comparison flyer gives an overall .94–.96 range. Both remain visible. The flyer gives 13.2% bilingual participants with English primary, not bilingual norms for any language. Accuracy at SS85 is .91/.81 for Sounds-in-Words. The .70–1.00 scorer range in the comparison flyer is in the GFTA-2 column and is not assigned to GFTA-3.

**KLPA-3.** The norm total 1,500 and 39 states are visible in the technical graphic. Retest .94 in the flyer differs from .92/.93 and unusual task labels in the graphic. Scorer ranges .98–1.00 versus .96–1.00 also differ. The SS85 accuracy pair agrees at .93/.83; lower-cutoff rows disagree. These are recorded as publisher-source conflicts.

**CAAP-2.** Norm n1,486, age 2:6–11:11, and the 15–20-minute inventory time are supported. Training reports scorer agreement 1.00 for the Articulation Inventory and >.99 for the Phonological Process Checklist. Retest significance p<.01 supplies no coefficient. Concurrent correlations .789–.948 with older GFTA/KLPA editions are not sensitivity/specificity.

**TNL-2.** Current publisher text differs on the lower age bound (4 versus 5); the finder retains the disclosed conflict. SALT's 778-child reference sample drawn from standardization is not established as the entire norm sample. A 2022 original study reports manual developmental subtest alpha .81 comprehension/.87 production and cohort scoring agreement 90.65–93.84%. Those percentages are not a retest coefficient or diagnostic accuracy. Public total-score retest and matched diagnostic-accuracy estimates remain gaps.

**OASES.** S/T/A forms cover 7–12, 13–17, and 18+ and have 60/80/100 items. The 2006 adult-development section alphas .92–.97 use n173; adult retest r .90–.97 uses n14 over 10–14 days. Neither is pooled evidence for all current child and adult forms. Percentage agreement and correlations are kept distinct.

**WAB-R.** Added AQ retest Pearson r .973, LQ .940 (n10), CQ .915 (n8), two weeks, chronic stroke, from Bond's 2019 thesis Table 4. This small research sample is distinct from manual norms. Rao's AQ mode concordance .942 concerns 19 PPA participants contributing 23 pairs, not 23 unique people or same-mode retest. A 2026 acute-stroke study reports 77% sensitivity/64% specificity at AQ93.7 in n171; it does not establish a universal cutoff. Original-WAB norm counts are identified as historical evidence.

**CLQT+.** Criterion-based interpretation, two administration paths, and age/time are supported. Development-study sample counts are separate cohorts. Public publisher descriptions do not supply exact current internal-consistency, retest, scorer, or matched diagnostic-accuracy coefficients; those remaining gaps are real within the inspected sources. Historical CLQT figures are not transferred automatically.

**CADL-3.** Publisher alpha .94 and retest .94 are reported. Norm n115 is not assigned to retest because the public retest summary does not give its sample, interval, or statistic. The public summary does not supply a matched diagnostic-accuracy pair.

**CASL-2.** Manual introduction reports internal consistency .85–.99 tests/.95–.99 indexes; two-week retest .73–.94/.88–.96; scorer ICC .86–.97. Detailed technical-table denominators are unavailable. The excerpt was personally read through indexed text; its direct CDN PDF returned 404 on this audit date, now disclosed in the source access field. Remote-equivalence results are a separate mode-agreement record. The GLAI SS85 .74/.84 pair concerns the mixed clinical validation sample; the .92/.92 pair comes from a more restricted moderate/severe sample and is not substituted. GLAI takes 30–45 minutes at ages 3–6 and 45–60 at 7–21; individual tests take 5–10 minutes.

**OWLS-II.** Oral Form A covers 3–21:11, written scales 5–21:11; Form B starts at 5. Time is per scale. The UNSW review supplies clinical internal reliability ≥.95/≥.93 and scorer ICCs .93–.96 by scale/form. Its n117 range .73–.94 appears in a paragraph whose method labeling is ambiguous; the site explicitly avoids presenting it as an established retest coefficient. Alternate-form .67–.96 (median .78; n319) is separate. Matched sensitivity/specificity remains unavailable.

**REEL-4.** Norm n1,019 and birth–36 months are supported. Publisher average reliability >.90 has no named method or score-specific coefficient, so it is labeled a general summary. Exact retest and diagnostic-accuracy values remain unavailable. Accuracy values for a different screening test in a study using REEL are not transferred to REEL-4.

**Bayley-III.** Review gives norm n1,700 and Language Composite split-half .93. Retest .67–.94 spans Cognitive/Language/Motor across n197 and is not a language-only range. Publisher/review norm-collection dates disagree. The current publisher identifies the edition as discontinued; the 30–90-minute time covers the battery, not an isolated language task. Correlation with WPPSI-IV is convergent validity. Language-specific scorer and matched diagnostic-accuracy gaps remain.

**Arizona-4.** Norm n3,192, age 1:6–21:11, and 5–20 minutes per articulation task are supported. Prahl and Schuele's 2022 original article reports manual internal-consistency .90–.97 and retest .96. Retest sample/interval are not supplied there. The 50-child speech-sound-disorder clinical sample is distinct from the norm total and is not treated as a complete diagnostic-accuracy denominator.

**KSPT.** McCauley and Strand's original 2008 review reports norm groups 447 typically developing and 263 with disordered speech. Two subtests have reported retest ≥.90; exact coefficients, n, and interval are not reproduced. That does not establish accuracy for childhood apraxia of speech. The 2026 combined manual format is not evidence of new norms for the original 1995 test.

**PLS-5 Spanish.** Spanish norm n1,150 is independent of English n1,400. Review gives normative split-half AC .80–.94, EC .80–.95, Total .87–.97; retest n193 with Total ≥.90 in three age groups and AC/EC below .90. Scorer .99/.99 involved rescoring 200 protocols. The reported .85/.88 accuracy pair lacks an explicit PLS cutoff and complete validation denominator in the review. A ≤77 threshold selected a reference group using an earlier unidentified measure and is not a PLS diagnostic cutoff.

**CELF-4 Spanish.** U.S. norm n1,019. Review normative alpha .65–.97 and split-half .62–.98 differ from clinical-group estimates. Retest corrected r .52–.93 uses n132, 7–30 days (mean 14). Fourteen trained raters are not 14 children. The 2018 cohort's validation n293 is distinct from its n656 parent cohort; at SS85 specificity is 65%, sensitivity 93% in Results versus 94% in Abstract. The paper's quoted manual .96/.87 pair is separate. Exploratory SS78 .86/.80 is study-specific. Current checked publisher text does not provide a completion time; the English-edition time is not substituted.

## Additional numerical check

The primary agent personally rechecked Petersen et al. (2020), including Table 3: 31 bilingual Spanish/English children, 21 typical/10 language-disordered, ages 5:9–9:7. Combined dynamic-assessment predictors yield 90% sensitivity/90.5% specificity with linear discriminant analysis and 100%/95.2% with logistic regression. These are small-sample research classification findings, not psychometrics for a commercial test. [Original study](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2019_LSHSS-18-0129).
